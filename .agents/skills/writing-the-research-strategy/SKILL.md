# Writing the Research Strategy

Verified 2026-10-04 against NIH Grants and Funding pages and the NSF PAPPG.
Sources are listed at the end.

The Research Strategy is where reviewers check the claims of the Aims page.
Write it against the review criteria reviewers will use. The science is the
researcher's. An agent shapes, cross-checks, and finds gaps.

## The rules

- **Required.** The Research Strategy has three parts, in order:
  Significance, Innovation, and Approach, per the NIH Application Guide.
- **Required.** Page limits come from the NIH page limits table, unless the
  NOFO sets others. Common limits: 12 pages for an R01, 6 for an R21 or R03.
  See `assembling-required-documents` for the full list.
- **Required.** A renewal adds a Progress Report inside the page limit. A
  resubmission adds a one-page Introduction outside it, per the Application
  Guide. See
  `responding-to-reviews-and-resubmitting`.
- **Required.** Follow the NOFO's own review criteria. A NOFO may add
  criteria or change the emphasis.

## The Simplified Review Framework

**Required.** For most research project grants with due dates on or after
January 25, 2025, NIH reviewers use three factors (NOT-OD-24-010):

| Factor | Old criteria it combines | How it is judged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Importance of the Research | Significance, Innovation | Scored 1 to 9 |
| 2. Rigor and Feasibility | Approach | Scored 1 to 9 |
| 3. Expertise and Resources | Investigators, Environment | "Appropriate" or "Identify need for additional expertise and/or resources"; not scored |

The overall impact score reflects all three factors and the additional
review criteria. The framework applies to research project grants such as
R01, R03, R15, R21, R33, R34, U01, and others named in the notice. It does
not apply to fellowships, career development awards, or training grants.
Fellowships have their own revised criteria, per NOT-OD-24-107. Check the
NOFO.

**Required.** Since the framework took effect, inclusion plans for women,
minorities, and individuals across the lifespan are judged under Factor 2,
per NOT-OD-24-010.

NIH made Factor 3 a two-way choice "to reduce the potential for general
scientific reputation to have an undue influence," per NOT-OD-24-010. The
biosketches and the Facilities and Other Resources attachment carry most of
its evidence. **Recommended.** Do not spend Research Strategy pages praising
the team or the institution. Show capability through the work.

## Significance

Reviewers ask whether the work addresses an important problem, and how
knowledge, methods, or practice will change if the aims succeed.

- **Required.** Address the rigor of prior research that the project builds
  on: its strengths and weaknesses, per the NIH rigor and reproducibility
  guidance. This replaced the older term "scientific premise."
- **Recommended.** State the gap in one sentence a reviewer can quote.
- **Recommended.** Cite current work. A significance section with no recent
  citations reads as out of touch.

For a structured literature search, see
`using-preliminary-data-and-feasibility`.

## Innovation

Reviewers ask whether the work challenges current paradigms or uses new
concepts, methods, or tools, and whether the novelty matters.

- **Recommended.** Say what is new and why it matters, in two to four points.
- **Practice.** Innovation for its own sake scores poorly. Tie each new
  element to the gap in Significance.

## Approach

Reviewers judge whether the design and methods are rigorous, feasible, and
appropriate to the aims. This is the factor that most often sinks an
application.

**Recommended.** Organize by aim. For each aim, give:

1. Rationale and, where relevant, preliminary data.
2. Experimental design and methods.
3. Rigor: how bias is reduced. Randomization, blinding, replicates, power,
   pre-specified analysis, and how missing data are handled.
4. Expected outcomes and how they will be interpreted.
5. Potential pitfalls and alternative approaches.
6. Benchmarks for success, and a timeline across aims.

### Rigor requirements

- **Required.** Describe how the design achieves robust and unbiased
  results, per NIH's rigor and reproducibility policy as stated in the
  Application Guide.
- **Required.** Address relevant biological variables, such as sex, age,
  and weight, in vertebrate animal and human studies. Justify studying only
  one sex, per NOT-OD-15-102.
- **Required.** Describe methods to authenticate key biological and
  chemical resources in a separate attachment, not in the Research Strategy,
  per the Application Guide. It is not scored, but reviewers comment on it.
- **Recommended.** State the power calculation and its inputs. See
  `using-preliminary-data-and-feasibility`.
- **Recommended.** Say which analyses are pre-specified and which are
  exploratory. A decision log from exploratory work supports this; see
  `using-preliminary-data-and-feasibility`.

### Feasibility

- **Recommended.** Show that the team has the data, samples, participants,
  and methods. Point to preliminary data, letters, and core facilities.
- **Recommended.** Show that the timeline fits the project period and the
  person months on the budget. See `budgeting-a-proposal`.
- **Recommended.** For data from a biobank, registry, or health system, name
  the access path and its approval steps. See
  `assembling-required-documents`.

## Additional review criteria

**Required.** These affect the overall impact score but are not separately
scored, per NOT-OD-24-010 and the NOFO:

- Protections for human subjects.
- Vertebrate animals.
- Biohazards.
- For resubmissions, renewals, and revisions, the response to prior review
  or progress made.

**Required.** Additional review considerations, such as authentication of
key resources and the budget and period of support, are noted by reviewers
but do not affect the score. Peer reviewers are not asked to comment on the
Data Management and Sharing Plan; institute program staff assess it, per
NIH's DMS plan page.

**Observed 2026-10-04 on NIH pages.** Through the January 2027 councils, NIH
study sections discuss fewer applications, and summary statements are
shorter, per NOT-OD-26-114. A new "competitive but not discussed" category
stays eligible for funding.

The human subjects sections live outside the Research Strategy. See
`assembling-required-documents`.

## NSF Project Description

- **Required.** Fifteen pages unless the solicitation says otherwise, per
  the PAPPG.
- **Required.** Include a separate section labeled "Broader Impacts" in the
  Project Description, per the PAPPG.
- **Required.** Include Results from Prior NSF Support for any PI or co-PI
  with NSF funding in the past five years, per the PAPPG.
- NSF reviewers judge Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts, and the five
  elements the PAPPG lists for each. Check the solicitation for added
  criteria.

## Checklist an agent can run on a draft

Report each item as met, not met, or unclear, quoting the text that shows it.

1. Significance, Innovation, and Approach appear in order, with those
   headings.
2. The draft fits the page limit for the activity code and NOFO.
3. Rigor of prior research is discussed, with strengths and weaknesses.
4. Each aim on the Aims page has a matching Approach section.
5. Each aim names design, methods, analysis, expected outcomes, pitfalls,
   and alternatives.
6. Bias reduction is explicit: randomization, blinding, or their absence
   justified.
7. Biological variables, including sex, are addressed or justified.
8. A power or sample size rationale exists for each hypothesis test.
9. A timeline or milestones table exists.
10. Every preliminary result cited exists in the researcher's material.
11. No paragraph argues investigator reputation or institutional prestige
    instead of showing capability.
12. Terms, aims numbering, and figures match the Aims page.

## Use of AI

**Required.** NIH will not consider applications "substantially developed by
AI" to be original ideas of applicants, per NOT-OD-25-132. An agent checks
and organizes. The researcher writes.

## Keep this file current

- Re-read NOT-OD-24-010 and the NIH review framework page each review. Watch

- Re-read the rigor and reproducibility page each review.
- Re-read the NSF PAPPG chapter on the Project Description each new PAPPG.
