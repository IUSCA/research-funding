# Responding to reviews and resubmitting

Verified 2026-10-04 against NIH Grants and Funding pages, the NSF PAPPG, and
IU Research pages. Sources are listed at the end.

Reviews are evidence about how the proposal read, not a verdict on the idea.
Read them, wait a day, then decide with the program officer. The response
is the researcher's. An agent organizes the critiques and checks the draft.

## Read the summary statement

An NIH summary statement holds each reviewer's critique, organized by review
factor, and, for discussed applications, a resume of the discussion.

- **Required.** Under the Simplified Review Framework, critiques address
  Importance of the Research and Rigor and Feasibility, each scored 1 to 9,
  and Expertise and Resources, marked "Appropriate" or "Identify need for
  additional expertise and/or resources," per NOT-OD-24-010. See `writing-the-research-strategy`.
- **Observed 2026-10-04 on NIH pages.** Through the January 2027 councils,
  study sections discuss fewer applications, and summary statements are
  shorter, per NOT-OD-26-114. An application marked "competitive but not
  discussed" remains eligible for funding.
- **Observed 2026-10-04 on NIH pages.** From the January 2026 councils,
  institutes "will not rely on funding paylines," per NIH's unified funding
  strategy. A percentile alone no longer says whether an application will
  be funded.

**Recommended.** Sort every critique into a table before deciding anything:

| Critique | Reviewer(s) | Factor | Kind | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <quote or paraphrase> | 1, 3 | Rigor and Feasibility | Fixable / misread / fundamental | <plan> |

A "misread" critique means the text failed to communicate. Fix the text, not
the reviewer.

## Decide: resubmit, revise as new, or move on

**Recommended.** Email the program officer after the summary statement is
released. Ask whether the institute might still fund the application,
whether a resubmission is advisable, and what the panel's main concern was.

| Situation | Usual choice |
| --- | --- |
| Concerns are fixable, and the program officer is encouraging | Resubmit as an A1 |
| The program officer says the application is still under consideration | Wait for council |
| The core idea was criticized, or the field moved | Rework and submit as new |
| An unsuccessful A1 | Submit as new, substantially changed, or move on |
| Wrong study section or institute | Resubmit with a cover letter request, or submit elsewhere |

## NIH resubmission rules

**Required.** NIH accepts "only a single resubmission (A1)" of an
application, per NIH's resubmission policy page and NOT-OD-18-197.

**Required.** The A1 must be submitted "within 37 months of the new,
renewal, or revision application it follows," per NIH's resubmission page.

**Required.** An A1 includes an Introduction of "one page or less," per
NIH's resubmission page and page limits table. It summarizes the
substantial additions, deletions, and changes, and responds to the issues
and criticism in the summary statement.

**Required.** "After an unsuccessful resubmission (A1), you may submit the
idea as a new application," with no Introduction and no marking of changes,
per NIH's resubmission page.

**Required.** A resubmission uses the resubmission due dates, such as March
5, July 5, and November 5 for an R01, per the NIH standard due dates page.
`planning-a-proposal` has the table.

**Required.** A resubmission counts toward NIH's limit of six applications
per PD/PI per calendar year, per NOT-OD-25-132.

## Write the Introduction

**Recommended.** Use this order:

1. One sentence of thanks, then the main concerns in the panel's terms.
2. For each major concern: the critique, quoted or summarized briefly, then
   the response and where in the application it is addressed.
3. Smaller changes, grouped.
4. Any new preliminary data, in one line, with its location.

**Recommended.** Be specific and unargumentative. Concede what was right.
Where a reviewer misread, say what the application now says more clearly.

**Required.** Follow the Application Guide on marking changes in the body,
and on what an Introduction may and may not contain. Check the current
instructions for each submission.

### Checklist an agent can run on a draft Introduction

1. It fits on one page in the required font and margins.
2. Every critique in the sorting table is answered or deliberately grouped.
3. Each response points to where the change is in the application.
4. No response argues with the reviewer's competence.
5. Changes claimed in the Introduction appear in the Research Strategy.
6. New preliminary data named are the researcher's own and real.

## NSF

**Required.** NSF has no formal resubmission type. "A declined proposal may
be resubmitted, but only after it has undergone substantial revision," and
NSF treats it as new, per PAPPG 24-1 Chapter IV.

**Required.** NSF may return without review a resubmission that "has not
clearly taken into account the major comments or concerns" of the prior
review, per PAPPG 24-1 Chapter IV.

**Recommended.** NSF has no Introduction. Address the prior reviews within
the Project Description, briefly, when the program officer advises it.

## Foundations

**Practice.** Foundations rarely give reviews. Ask the program officer what
would make a future proposal stronger, and whether reapplying is welcome.

## Support at IU

- As of 2026-10-04, IU Research runs an External Resubmission Grant of up
  to $50,000, open through June 4, 2027 with quarterly reviews, per its
  internal funding page.
  It pays for data or proof of concept a resubmission needs. "Proposals that
  were not reviewed or scored are not eligible." **Open item.** Whether a
  not-discussed NIH application qualifies. Ask IU Research.
- Indiana CTSI Project Development Teams advise on revising a submission and
  can award small pilot funds, "typically up to $15,000," per the PDT page.
- RDS Proposal Development Services reviews resubmission strategy. See
  `getting-help-with-proposals`.
