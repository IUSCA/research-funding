# Finding funding opportunities

Verified 2026-10-04 against NIH, NSF, Grants.gov, and IU Research pages.
Sources are listed at the end.

The funding opportunity is the contract for one application. Find the right
one, then read every word of it. It overrides every general rule in these
skills.

## Search

| Where | Finds | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grants.gov and Simpler Grants.gov | All federal NOFOs, NIH's included | The single official source for NIH NOFOs, per NOT-OD-25-143 |
| NIH Guide for Grants and Contracts | NIH notices that change policy or amend NOFOs | Notices only, since NOFOs moved to Grants.gov |
| NIH parent announcements | Investigator-initiated NOFOs for common activity codes | The usual route for an unsolicited R01 or R21 |
| NIH Highlighted Topics | Institute interests published in place of many narrow NOFOs | Apply through a parent announcement |
| CSR Assisted Referral Tool | Likely study sections for pasted text | Helps choose study-section language |
| NIH RePORTER | Funded NIH projects | Who funds similar work, and which study section reviewed it |
| NIH RePORTER Matchmaker | Similar projects for pasted text | Suggests institutes, program officers, and study sections |
| NSF funding search | NSF programs and solicitations | Most NSF programs have a standing solicitation |
| NSF award search | Funded NSF awards | Which program funds similar work |
| Pivot (Pivot-RP) | Federal, foundation, and other funders | IU-licensed; "All IU faculty, staff and students can access Pivot" |
| IU curated funding opportunities | Opportunities IU Research has screened | IU Research page |
| Foundation Directory Online | Foundations | Through IU Libraries |
| InfoReady | IU limited submissions and internal funds | See below |

**Recommended.** Search RePORTER for three to five funded projects close to
the idea. Note the institutes, activity codes, and study sections that
recur. That is better evidence of fit than a keyword match on a NOFO.

## Choose the mechanism

These are typical NIH shapes. The NOFO sets the actual limits.

| Activity code | Typical use | Typical limits (check the NOFO) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R01 | A discrete, multi-year project | Up to five years; no set budget cap; 12-page Research Strategy |
| R21 | Exploratory or developmental work; preliminary data not required | Two years; $275,000 combined direct costs, no more than $200,000 in a year, per the parent R21; 6 pages |
| R03 | Small, self-contained project | Two years; small budget; 6 pages |
| K awards | Mentored or independent career development | Career-stage eligibility rules. For due dates from October 12, 2027, NIH consolidates K awards into KR1, KC1, KS1, and KT1/R00, drops the DMS Plan for K applications, and bars K funds from paying for a clinical trial, per NOT-OD-26-098. |
| F awards | Pre- and postdoctoral fellowships | Training plan and sponsor |
| U awards | Cooperative agreements with NIH involvement | Usually a specific NOFO |

**Recommended.** Choose an R21 for work that is truly exploratory, not as a
small R01. Reviewers criticize an R21 that reads as an R01 squeezed short.

**Observed 2026-10-04 on NIH pages.** NIH is cutting narrow NOFOs and
steering investigator-initiated work to parent announcements, with Highlighted
Topics to signal institute interest. Since the January 2026 councils,
institutes "will not rely on funding paylines," per NIH's unified funding
strategy. A percentile no longer predicts funding by itself. Ask the program
officer.

**Required.** NIH limits each PD/PI to "six new, renewal, resubmission, or
revision applications" per calendar year, with exceptions, per
NOT-OD-25-132. Count every application where the person is PD/PI or
multiple PI.

## Read the funding opportunity

Read the whole document. Record the answers in the plan template from
`planning-a-proposal`.

1. **Eligibility.** Institution type, PI career stage, citizenship, and any
   limit on applications per institution.
2. **Due dates.** Standard dates, special dates, or an expiration date. Note
   the time and time zone.
3. **Clinical trials.** "Required," "optional," or "not allowed." Applying
   to the wrong type gets an application withdrawn.
4. **Budget and period.** Caps, modular rules, and cost sharing.
5. **Attachments, required and optional.** Note any that override the
   general guide.
6. **Page limits.** Any that differ from the standard table.
7. **Review criteria.** The factors and any added criteria. Read this before
   writing.
8. **Contacts.** The scientific, peer review, and grants management
   contacts.
9. **Notices.** Search the NIH Guide for notices that amend the NOFO.
10. **Letters of intent.** NIH no longer requests or accepts them, per
    NOT-OD-26-019. Other sponsors may still require one.

**Required.** Where the NOFO and the general Application Guide differ, the
NOFO wins, per the NIH Application Guide.

## Limited submissions

**Required.** When a sponsor limits the number of applications from one
institution, IU selects them internally. "InfoReady lists IU's current
limited submission opportunities as well as instructions for submitting
internal applications," per the IU Research limited submissions page.

- Subscribe to the weekly listserv by emailing limsub@iu.edu.
- Internal deadlines are set per opportunity in InfoReady, often months
  before the sponsor's.
- **Recommended.** Check InfoReady as soon as a NOFO names an institutional
  limit.

## NSF

- **Required.** Proposals follow the PAPPG unless the solicitation says
  otherwise. NSF 24-1 applies to proposals "submitted or due on or after May
  20, 2024," with supplements NSF 26-200 and NSF 26-202 changing award
  terms.
- **Required.** Concurrent duplicate or substantially similar proposals to
  more than one NSF program are returned, per PAPPG Chapter I.
- **Recommended.** Email the program officer with a one-page summary. NSF
  program officers expect it.
- NSF reviews for Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts. NSF's 2025
  priorities page asks investigators to "prioritize the first six broader
  impacts goals." See `writing-the-research-strategy`.

## Foundations

- **Practice.** Many foundations ask for a letter of inquiry first and invite
  full proposals.
- **Required.** Some foundations require coordination through IU's Corporate
  and Foundation Relations, per IU Research's corporate and foundation page.
  See `routing-and-submitting-at-iu`.
- **Required.** Foundation calls set their own indirect cost limits. For
  example, the Alzheimer's Association caps indirect costs "at 10 percent of
  total direct costs" for one program, per its program page. See
  `budgeting-a-proposal`.

## IU internal funds for preliminary data

IU Research and Indiana CTSI run seed and pilot programs that can produce
preliminary data. As of 2026-10-04 they include an IU Research Seed Grant,
the IU 2030 Emerging Frontiers Grant, an External Resubmission Grant, and
Indiana CTSI Project Development Team pilots. Dates change each cycle. Check
the IU Research opportunities page and the CTSI funding page.
